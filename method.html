01 / THE CONCEPT

What are Control Candlesticks?

On a price chart, a candlestick shows how price moved during a period. Traders familiar with ICT or Smart Money Concepts may use order blocks, horizontal levels and other chart references to anticipate reactions. The challenge is deciding which areas show meaningful pressure and are worth watching.

Control Candlesticks — shortened to Control Candles or CCs — are candlesticks identified by TCJ SVC when unusually strong buying or selling pressure stands out. They are not identified by candlestick size alone. Price movement, strong volume and Force Index (price × volume) help identify significant activity.

Think of a CC as a footprint left by a strong push in the market. Its price area becomes a reference point. Watch whether price respects or violates that area.

02 / WHY THE AREA MATTERS

Pressure doesn't only appear at swings.

A Control Candle does not have to form at a swing high or swing low. Significant pressure can appear during a move, even after a swing has already formed. TCJ SVC uses defined rules to identify the activity instead of relying only on visual judgment.

The reference point helps you focus on how price behaves next. It does not guarantee that the area will hold.

03 / READING CONTROL

What does Control mean?

Bullish Control

Buyers have control while price holds above the bullish Control Candle. A confirmed close below the candle body invalidates that bullish control.

Bearish Control

Sellers have control while price holds below the bearish Control Candle. A confirmed close above the candle body invalidates that bearish control.

No Control means the system has no valid established bullish or bearish control to report. Identifying a pressure area and establishing directional control are separate steps.

Control gives you context, not an automatic entry signal. Examine the chart and define your trade’s invalidation before entering.

04 / PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

How to use the method

  1. Find a market. Use Zero Scanner to find candidates showing activity.
  2. Read the chart. Watch price around the Control Candle area: is it holding, testing or breaking through?
  3. Assess the setup. Define your entry, invalidation and target. If there is no clear setup, move on.

A scanner result is a reason to investigate, not a reason to enter automatically.
